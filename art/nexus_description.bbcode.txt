[size=6][b][color=#e8905a]Wraithguard Toolkit[/color][/b][/size]
[i]Sort your custom mods into your OpenMW load order - without ever touching your curated Modding-OpenMW.com list.[/i]
[center][youtube]yx7eFfxFLlU[/youtube][/center]
[line]
[b][color=#e8905a]The problem[/color][/b]
You built a carefully-tuned mod list from [url=https://modding-openmw.com/]Modding-OpenMW.com[/url] with umo + MOMW Configurator. Then you added a few mods of your own. They need to slot into the load order - but running a whole-list sorter like PLOX would scramble the curated MOMW order, which MOMW [b]explicitly warns against[/b].
[b][color=#e8905a]What it does[/color][/b]
Wraithguard Toolkit treats your existing [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] order as [b]frozen[/b] and only works out where [i]your[/i] additions belong - using the real mlox rules - then writes the result back as a corrected [font=Courier New]momw-customizations.toml[/font].
That's the durable fix: it [b]survives every future Configurator rebuild[/b], and your curated list is [b]never reordered[/b]. Only your mods move.
[b][color=#e8905a]Features[/color][/b]
• [b]Frozen curated order[/b] - sorts only your custom additions; the MOMW list is left exactly as it is.
• [b]Real mlox engine[/b] - ported from mlox and cross-checked against plox: [font=Courier New]*[/font]/[font=Courier New]?[/font] wildcards and the [font=Courier New]<VER>[/font] token, ordering that bridges over plugins you don't have, and [font=Courier New][Conflict]/[Requires]/[Note][/font] warnings that understand [font=Courier New]ALL/ANY/NOT/DESC[/font] plus the [font=Courier New][VER]/[SIZE]/[DESC][/font] functions.
• [b]plugin-order.yml aware[/b] - with your list name set, it tells curated-list plugins apart from your true additions and flags [color=#ffb454]redundant[/color], [color=#ffb454]orphan[/color], [color=#ffb454]needs-cleaning[/color], and base-order-drift issues.
• [b]Conflict detection, the xEdit way[/b] - a plugin → record-type → record tree and a [b]field-level diff[/b] carrying the two-axis conflict status modders know from xEdit, [b]colour-coded[/b] ([color=#7ee0a0]green = agree[/color], [color=#ffb454]amber = benign override[/color], [color=#ff6b6b]red = a losing edit[/color]). See which plugin wins, and why.
• [b]See the change, not just the bytes[/b] - compiled scripts are [b]disassembled[/b] rather than shown as base64; a conflicting mesh opens the actual [b]3D model[/b] side-by-side (our own NIF reader) and a texture the [b]decoded image[/b] (our own DDS/BC7/TGA decoder); and a [b]DIAL/INFO[/b] record reads as [b]dialogue[/b] - the line an NPC says, to whom, and under what conditions.
• [b]Merged Lands[/b] - merges the landscape edits a load order would otherwise silently discard (a function-by-function port of David Von Derau's Rust tool), with a slope limiter, curvature-weighted resolution, per-layer [font=Courier New].mergedlands.toml[/font] control, seam repair and conflict images.
• [b]Cell map[/b] - a heatmap of which mods touch which cells, rendered in-app or in your browser.
• [b]Resource (VFS) conflicts[/b] - the loose-file "Data" conflict view for your data paths, in load order.
• [b]Rule maker[/b] - author and test your own mlox rules in-app, from a conflict you found.
• [b]Health checks[/b] - master presence/order verification, a lint pass, save-file checks and timestamped backups.
• [b]tes3cmd frontend[/b] - clean plugins without leaving the app (Morrowind/Tribunal/Bloodmoon are never touched).
• [b]One-click mods-folder scan[/b] - point it at your custom mods folder and it builds the subset for you.
• [b]Drag-and-drop GUI + full CLI[/b] - reorder rows, with a live, color-coded log ([color=#7ee0a0]green = inserted/moved[/color], [color=#ffb454]orange = heads-up[/color], red = error).
• [b]Fully themeable[/b] - [color=#bd93f9]Dracula[/color], [color=#a6e22e]Monokai[/color], [color=#61afef]Atom One Dark[/color], [color=#fabd2f]Gruvbox[/color] built in, and you can import your own or any [b]base16[/b] scheme. Themes the whole app, and switching applies instantly to every open window.
• [b]Opt out what you don't want[/b] - disable plugins/paths (multi-select); anything already in your cfg is removed durably via [font=Courier New]removeContent[/font]/[font=Courier New]removeData[/font].
• [b]Safe by default[/b] - dry-run preview, timestamped backups, and it can either emit a corrected TOML (recommended) or patch [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] directly.
• [b]Cross-platform[/b] - Windows, Linux and macOS (Intel + Apple Silicon). Prebuilt binaries for all three, or run from source (pure Python + tkinter).
[b][color=#e8905a]New in 3.1.X[/color][/b]
• [b]Merged Lands[/b] - the landscape merge, ported function-by-function from David Von Derau's Rust tool with our own slope limiter, curvature-weighted resolution and digest-based cleaning. Terrain edits a load order would otherwise discard are merged into one plugin, with per-layer control, corner-then-edge seam repair and conflict images. [font=Courier New]MERGED_LANDS.md[/font] accounts for every function and records where we diverge.
• [b]An xEdit-style conflict view[/b] - Check Conflicts grew a plugin → type → record tree and a field-level diff carrying the two-axis conflict status (what each plugin does, and what happens overall), colour-coded the way modders read xEdit. Mesh and texture conflicts open the real 3D mesh or decoded texture side-by-side, and DIAL/INFO records read as dialogue.
• [b]Our own NIF, DDS/BC7 and BSA readers[/b] - so the diff can show meshes and textures with no heavyweight binary dependency, each verified byte-for-byte against a reference implementation (documented in [font=Courier New]NIF_PROVENANCE.md[/font]).
• [b]Prebuilt binaries for every platform[/b] - a Windows [font=Courier New].exe[/font], a Linux binary and a macOS [font=Courier New].app[/font] (Intel + Apple Silicon) are autobuilt in CI and attached to each release.
• [b]Verified, not asserted[/b] - 4,500+ automated tests and a differential baseline pinning sorting behaviour against a real load order.
[b][color=#e8905a]Requirements[/color][/b]
• OpenMW with a MOMW list installed via the [url=https://modding-openmw.com/guides/auto/]Automatic Install Guide[/url] (umo + MOMW Configurator).
• Python 3.10+ with tkinter (bundled with the python.org installers on Windows/macOS; on Linux e.g. [font=Courier New]sudo apt install python3-tk[/font]). [i]Not needed if you use the prebuilt binary for your platform.[/i]
• mlox rule files ([font=Courier New]mlox_base.txt[/font] / [font=Courier New]mlox_user.txt[/font]).
• [i]Optional:[/i] MOMW's [font=Courier New]plugin-order.yml[/font], and PyYAML (a built-in parser is used if it's absent).
[b][color=#e8905a]How to use[/color][/b]
See the included [b]QUICKSTART[/b] for the 5-minute version. In short:
1. Point it at your [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] and add [font=Courier New]mlox_base.txt[/font] + [font=Courier New]mlox_user.txt[/font].
2. Build your subset: click [b]Scan...[/b] and pick your custom mods folder (or load your [font=Courier New]momw-customizations.toml[/font]).
3. Set your [b]list name[/b] (e.g. [font=Courier New]total-overhaul[/font]) and point [b]plugin-order.yml[/b] at MOMW's file.
4. Click [b]1. Sort[/b], review the panels and log, then [b]2. Export[/b] a corrected [font=Courier New]momw-customizations.toml[/font].
5. Drop that file next to your [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] and re-run MOMW Configurator. Done - your mods sort into place on every rebuild.
[b][color=#e8905a]What it will NOT do[/color][/b]
• Reorder your curated MOMW list - ever.
• Write anything until you tell it to (dry-run is on by default; backups are made first).
• Guarantee your customizations are conflict-free. It helps you [i]see[/i] and [i]place[/i] them - the rest is on you. Customizations are not supported by the MOMW team.

[b][color=#e8905a]The NIF reader, and why it is written rather than imported[/color][/b]
[font=Courier New]wraithguard/nif/[/font] reads Morrowind meshes with our own code. That is a licence decision, taken deliberately and recorded here so it is not revisited by accident:
• [b]pyFFI[/b] - LGPL. The obvious Python choice. This tool ships as a PyInstaller onefile binary, and statically bundling an LGPL library carries a relinking obligation that does not fit that distribution.
• [b]nifly[/b] - GPL-3.0. [b]io_scene_mw[/b] (Greatness7's Morrowind Blender plugin) - GPL-3.0, Python, and scoped to exactly this game, which makes it the most tempting option by far. [b]NifSkope[/b] - GPL.
• [b]nif.xml[/b] (the NifTools format description) - in a GPL-3.0 repository whose own licence status is [url=https://github.com/niftools/nifxml/issues/86]disputed upstream[/url]. An unresolved licence is worse than one that clearly says no, so it was not used as a source either.
• [b]niflib[/b] - [b]BSD-3[/b], and therefore the permissively-licensed reference to consult if a layout ever needs checking against an implementation.
[b][color=#e8905a]three.js - bundled, not merely referenced[/color][/b]
[font=Courier New]wraithguard/viz/assets/three.cjs[/font] is [b]three.js r185, unmodified[/b], MIT licensed, with its licence text beside it as [font=Courier New]three-LICENSE.txt[/font]. It is the first third-party [i]source[/i] this project ships, as distinct from the Python packages PyInstaller already collects, so it is called out here rather than left to be discovered in a build.
It is the [b]CommonJS[/b] build, which looks like an odd choice until the constraint is stated: modern three.js ships ESM only, split across [font=Courier New]three.module.min.js[/font] and [font=Courier New]three.core.min.js[/font], and [b]ES module scripts do not load from [font=Courier New]file://[/font][/b] - the origin is [font=Courier New]null[/font] and the CORS check fails. The viewer pages are written to disk and opened in a browser, so no ESM packaging can work. The CJS build is one self-contained file with no [font=Courier New]require()[/font] of its own and runs as a classic script behind a three-line shim.
The orbit controls in the page are ours, not three.js's [font=Courier New]OrbitControls.js[/font], because that imports the bare specifier [font=Courier New]'three'[/font] and would pull ESM back into a page built specifically to avoid it.
[font=Courier New]wraithguard/nif/bsa.py[/font] reads Morrowind's archives, and is ours for the same reasons again. [b]bethesda-structs[/b] (MIT, Stephen Bunn) via [b]BSAFileExtractor[/b] (MIT, Pierre GAMBIER) would have been licence-compatible, so this was an engineering call rather than a legal one: it pulls in [font=Courier New]construct[/font], [font=Courier New]multidict[/font], [font=Courier New]attrs[/font] and [font=Courier New]lz4[/font] - the last with a compiled extension - ships a 49 MB tree covering Fallout and Skyrim record formats this project will never touch, and every archive in its own test suite is the [i]post-Morrowind[/i] BSA format, which shares an extension with Morrowind's and nothing else. Morrowind's layout is a header and three tables. Neither project was read for the format; it was implemented from the public description and checked against a shipped archive with [font=Courier New]tools/check_bsa.py[/font].
[font=Courier New]wraithguard/images/[/font] is ours for the same reasons and by the same method. Pillow would decode these textures, but it is a large binary dependency in a PyInstaller onefile build. It was used instead as an [b]oracle[/b]: the corpus textures decode byte-for-byte identically to it, BC7 matches on 19,380 random blocks across every mode and partition, and every PNG we write reads back through it unchanged. Used but not read, exactly as with NifSkope.
[b]pydds was evaluated for BC7 and rejected on licence.[/b] It is the closest technical fit - DDS decompression bindings including BC7, which is precisely what was wanted - and it is [b]GPLv3-or-later[/b], which would relicense this entire project. That decision needed no technical argument at all. Two further facts made it moot anyway: it [i]depends on[/i] Pillow rather than replacing it, so adopting it would have added a dependency rather than removed one; and it is a compiled extension at version 0.0.8, marked alpha.
[font=Courier New]quicktex[/font] (Apache-2.0) would have been licence-compatible and remains the option if a hand-written BC7 decoder ever proves too slow. It was not needed: ours matches an independent implementation exactly, and a viewer decodes one texture on demand rather than a collection.
The BC7 tables come from the [b]published format specification[/b] - Khronos's OpenGL BPTC specification and Microsoft's Direct3D 11 documentation - not from any implementation. [font=Courier New]NIF_PROVENANCE.md[/font] records how that was verified, and why transcribing six hundred numbers needed a cross-check rather than a unit test.
[b][color=#e8905a]Greatness7 relicensed the es3 library so this project could use it[/color][/b]
On 28 July 2026, [b]Greatness7[/b] - author of the Morrowind Blender Plugin and of [font=Courier New]Greatness7/tes3[/font] - offered to relicense the NIF library inside [font=Courier New]io_scene_mw[/font], and then did it: [url=https://github.com/Greatness7/io_scene_mw/commit/cbe18b558299e14ecd959183e3cf9ea096fe95df]cbe18b5[/url] adds an MIT [font=Courier New]LICENSE[/font] to [font=Courier New]lib/es3/[/font]. [font=Courier New]Greatness7/tes3[/font] was already MIT.
That was an unprompted act of generosity toward a project that had spent months carefully working around his code, and it is worth naming plainly. The relicensed library is [font=Courier New]lib/es3/[/font] [b]only[/b]; the rest of [font=Courier New]io_scene_mw[/font] remains GPL-3.0 because Blender requires plugins to be, and this project respects that line. See [font=Courier New]NIF_PROVENANCE.md[/font] for the exact boundary.
Within an hour of reading [font=Courier New]tes3[/font], the cross-check had confirmed this project's hardest-won layout - the typed bounding box - and found a gap it could not have found alone: [font=Courier New]NiUnionBV[/font], a bound type no file in either corpus carries, which this reader would have refused. A second implementation sees what a corpus cannot.
[b]How each field layout was actually derived - and what was deliberately not read to derive it - is recorded in [font=Courier New]NIF_PROVENANCE.md[/font].[/b] That document is the companion to this one: this section says [i]why[/i] the reader is ours, and that one says [i]where every fact in it came from[/i], with the worked derivations so they can be re-run rather than taken on trust.
Going GPL-3.0 was considered seriously: it would unlock io_scene_mw, pyFFI, nifly and - the bigger prize - [b]OpenMW[/b], whose NIF loading, texture handling and [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] semantics this tool models from the outside. The project stays [b]MIT[/b] for now, so none of those were read for the reader's field layouts. They come from the publicly documented format, checked against real meshes with [font=Courier New]tools/check_nif_layouts.py[/font].
[b][color=#e8905a]Referenced for formats & behavior (GPL - no source copied)[/color][/b]
We read these projects to understand file formats and expected behavior. [b]No GPL source was copied into this tool[/b], so no copyleft obligations attach to it; the credit is one of gratitude and correctness.
• [b]OpenMW[/b] - GPLv3. The engine that makes modern Morrowind modding possible. Referenced for [font=Courier New]openmw.cfg[/font] semantics, the [font=Courier New].omwaddon[/font]/[font=Courier New].omwscripts[/font] Lua formats, and VFS ([font=Courier New]data=[/font]) resolution rules.
• [b]Mod Organizer 2[/b] - GPLv3. Referenced for the "Data" loose-file conflict concept behind our data-path (VFS) resource conflict checker.
• [b]MWSE[/b] - © NullCascade, Merzasphor, Greatness7 and contributors. [b]GPLv2.[/b] We read [font=Courier New]MWSE/OpCodes.h[/font] to [i]check[/i] our opcode table and found it agreed with MWEdit on all 533 opcodes they share. Because MWSE is copyleft and this tool is not, [b]no MWSE source was copied[/b]: the shipped table is built from MWEdit (MIT), our own corpus measurements, and [font=Courier New]customfunctions.dat[/font].
That last file needs saying precisely. [font=Courier New]customfunctions.dat[/font] is a [b]data file in MWEdit's own text format[/b], describing the MWSE / MW-Enhanced script functions so MWEdit can compile against them. It is installed by running the MWSE updater rather than being part of the MWSE source tree, and it is configuration for an MIT tool, not a copyleft header - so reading it does not bring GPLv2 obligations with it. It contributes [b]360 opcodes[/b] the base game has no equivalent for, which is what makes an MWSE-scripted mod disassemble instead of coming out as raw bytes.
It spells parameter types symbolically ([font=Courier New]Long | String[/font]) where [font=Courier New]Functions.dat[/font] uses hex flag words, and the mapping between the two was [b]derived, not copied[/b]: the two files describe 106 of the same functions, so correlating those pins each symbolic name to exactly one bit value. The result matches the [font=Courier New]FLAG_*[/font] constants already taken from MWEdit's MIT header, which is how we know the derivation is right.
Where the two files disagree - two renames ([font=Courier New]XDrop[/font]/[font=Courier New]XDropItem[/font], [font=Courier New]XEquip[/font]/[font=Courier New]XEquipItem[/font]) and 26 differing operand shapes - [b]the existing MWEdit-derived entry is kept[/b] and the disagreement printed rather than resolved silently. 25 of the 26 are the same call: MWEdit says [font=Courier New]String[/font] where customfunctions says [font=Courier New]Long | String[/font] for a filename or object id, and UESP's per-function pages document those parameters as strings, which settles it.
The 26th is a genuine error and is corrected: MWEdit gives [font=Courier New]XFileWriteFloat[/font] a single float operand with no filename, where customfunctions lists two and UESP documents [font=Courier New]xFileWriteFloat filename (string), value (float)[/font]. Corrections live in one small explicit table in the generator, each with its evidence.
• [b]MGE XE[/b] - GPLv3. Referenced alongside MWSE for the same cross-check; no source copied.
[b][color=#e8905a]Curated data & tooling[/color][/b]
• [b][url=https://modding-openmw.com/]Modding-OpenMW.com[/url] (MOMW)[/b] - the curated mod lists, the [font=Courier New]umo[/font] installer, the MOMW Configurator, and [font=Courier New]plugin-order.yml[/font] (the source of truth for which plugins belong to which list). This tool is designed specifically to [i]complement[/i] MOMW lists without ever reordering them. Customizations are not supported by the MOMW team.
• [b]tes3cmd[/b] - © 2016 John Moonsugar. MIT. ([url=https://github.com/john-moonsugar/tes3cmd/]github.com/john-moonsugar/tes3cmd[/url]) The plugin-maintenance Swiss-army knife distributed with the MOMW Tools Pack. Our [b]tes3cmd[/b] window is a front-end that stages plugins with their masters so tes3cmd works correctly on a multi-folder OpenMW VFS; we drive the real binary for [font=Courier New]clean[/font], and reimplement master-size resync in-app (tes3cmd's own sync corrupts headers on this layout). The safe-cleaning workflow (never cleaning the vanilla masters, cleaning masters before dependents) is adapted from the community "drag-and-drop" cleaning batch by RMWChaos, Pinkertonius, and Spirithawke.
[b][color=#e8905a]Documentation referenced (no code copied)[/color][/b]
• [b][url=https://en.uesp.net/]UESP[/url] - [i]Morrowind Mod:Mod File Format[/i].[/b] CC-BY-SA. The community's reference for the TES3 binary layout, and the source of [font=Courier New]wraithguard/tes3fields/schema.py[/font]: 46 record types, their subrecords, whether each is required, its declared width, and the named members inside the struct ones. What is taken is [b]format fact[/b] - a [font=Courier New]NPDT[/font] is 12 or 52 bytes; its first two are a uint16 Level - which describes Bethesda's file format rather than anyone's prose about it, and the generated module carries the attribution.
The schema is what lets the diff window say [i]what a field is[/i] rather than only what its value was, and what backs the [b]Format reference[/b] view beside a record diff. Nothing is guessed: where the tables are ambiguous the schema says so (a field with two documented layouts carries neither), and 56 of the parsed layouts are checked against the byte counts the same tables declare - all 56 agree, which is how we know the parse is right.
Also the source for the LAND, PGRD and script-record field documentation used when writing the binary decoders, alongside the MIT-licensed implementations credited above.
[b][color=#e8905a]yampt - Yet Another Morrowind Plugin Tools[/color][/b]
• [b]yampt[/b] - MIT, Copyright (c) 2016-2026 Rafał Wierzchoś. Licence text vendored at [font=Courier New]License/yampt/LICENSE[/font].
Four things are ported from yampt's C++, with its permission by licence:
• [font=Courier New]status.py[/font] - the two-axis conflict model, from [font=Courier New]yampt.core/source/scanner/record_conflict.cpp[/font] and the worst-of roll-up in [font=Courier New]yampt.editor/source/model/nav_tree_model.cpp[/font]. The names ([font=Courier New]conflict_all[/font], [font=Courier New]conflict_this[/font], "identical to master", "override wins") follow yampt, which in turn follows TES5Edit/xEdit's "Conflict Status All" / "Conflict Status This". The absence sentinel is ours: yampt uses a magic byte string because its values are C++ strings, and Python can just use an object.
• [font=Courier New]wraithguard/gui/pluginview.py[/font] - the plugin tree's shape, from [font=Courier New]yampt.editor/source/model/nav_tree_model.hpp[/font]: file node -> record-type group -> records, each level taking the worst status of its children on both axes. yampt follows xEdit here and so do we.
• [font=Courier New]align.py[/font] - matching the entries of a repeated field by content identity rather than by position, from [font=Courier New]yampt.core/source/decoder/content_alignment.cpp[/font]. yampt does this over raw subrecords with per-record anchor and key rules; working from tes3conv's decoded JSON means the entries arrive structured, so what is taken is the principle and the question it answers - what identifies an entry - not the subrecord machinery.
• [font=Courier New]patch/dialogue.py[/font] - resolving a topic into the order the engine reads it, from [font=Courier New]yampt.core/source/scanner/dial_info_align.cpp[/font]. Including the rule that matters most: a response whose [font=Courier New]PNAM[/font] names something not yet seen goes to the [b]end[/b] of the topic. (The [i]readable[/i] rendering of a dialogue record is separate - see Morrowind Dialog Explorer above.)
Verified rather than assumed: the port reproduces the file order of all 4,111 topics and 36,735 responses in Morrowind.esm, Tribunal.esm and Bloodmoon.esm exactly, from the [font=Courier New]prev_id[/font] chain alone.
